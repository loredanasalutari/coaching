Loredana Salutari — Coach for Professional Change

When work changes, so can we.

I work with professionals navigating career transitions, organizational change, and moments when their professional life no longer feels aligned.

Coaching is no longer my primary professional activity, but I remain open to occasional conversations where there is a good fit.

01 — About

From technology to people.

I spent more than 25 years working in the IT industry, in complex and regulated environments where technology, organizations, responsibility, hierarchy, and people constantly intersect.

Over time, I became increasingly interested in what happens to people when the professional environment around them changes: when roles disappear or evolve, when organizations restructure, or when a career that once made sense no longer feels aligned.

This eventually led me to life coaching, purpose-oriented coaching, Neuro-Linguistic Programming, and hypnosis.

Today, coaching is not my primary professional activity, but I remain open to working with people who are navigating significant professional transitions and want a space to think clearly about what comes next.

02 — Focus

Career transitions and change.

My coaching work has focused primarily on professionals going through periods of change: restructuring, role changes, uncertainty, loss of direction, or a growing sense that their work no longer fits who they are.

These situations are not always dramatic from the outside. Sometimes someone is still performing well, but feels increasingly disconnected from their role. Sometimes responsibilities and authority no longer correspond. Sometimes the question is simply whether to stay, leave, or find a different way of working.

I am particularly interested in the space between the individual and the organization — where professional identity, personal values, expectations, and organizational realities meet.

Clarity is often more useful than certainty.

03 — Approach

Making space to see what is actually happening.

I do not see coaching as motivation, advice, or telling someone what they should do.

My approach is conversational, structured, and adaptive. The aim is to slow a complex situation down enough to understand it: what is happening, what belongs to the surrounding system, what belongs to the individual, and what choices are genuinely available.

Depending on the person and the situation, I draw on analytical frameworks, purpose-oriented coaching, Neuro-Linguistic Programming, and guided conversational techniques, including hypnosis where appropriate.

These are tools rather than formulas. The work is shaped around the person, their circumstances, and the questions they are actually trying to answer.

What has changed?

What no longer fits?

What is within your control?

What options are actually available?

What would a coherent next step look like?

04 — Background

Experience inside complex systems.

Before moving into coaching, I spent more than 25 years in technology and organizational environments.

That experience shaped the way I understand professional change. Organizations have structures, incentives, constraints, informal rules, and competing interests. People have identities, values, expectations, responsibilities, and limits.

Difficult professional situations often emerge somewhere between those two realities.

I bring that perspective to coaching: respect for the individual, but also respect for the system in which that individual has to operate.
