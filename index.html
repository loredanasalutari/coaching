A professional journey through technology, organizations, coaching, and the human side of professional change.

01 — About me

From technology to people.

I spent more than 25 years working in the IT industry, in complex and regulated environments where technology, organizations, hierarchy, responsibility and people constantly intersect.

Over time, my work expanded into a different kind of question: what happens to people when the professional environment around them changes faster than they can make sense of it?

That question eventually led me to life coaching, purpose-oriented coaching, Neuro-Linguistic Programming and hypnosis.

02 — My work

A different perspective on work.

For several years, through Viauki, I worked with professionals experiencing restructuring, role changes, uncertainty, loss of direction and misalignment at work.

I was particularly interested in the situations that are difficult to name: when someone is still competent and functioning, but something no longer fits; when responsibilities and authority no longer correspond; when personal values and organizational expectations begin to pull in different directions.

My approach was never about motivation or telling people what they should do.

It was about creating enough space to understand what was happening — what belonged to the individual, what belonged to the surrounding system, and what choices were actually available.

Clarity is often more useful than certainty.
03 — Perspective

Systems, people, and the space between them.

My professional background taught me to look at systems rather than isolated events.

Organizations have structures, incentives, constraints and informal rules. People have identities, values, expectations and limits. Difficult professional situations often emerge in the space between the two.

This perspective continues to shape how I think about work, change and professional identity.

I am interested in the questions that arise when a career no longer follows the expected path:

What remains when a role changes?

How much of ourselves should we adapt to an environment?

When is it time to stay, and when is it time to leave?

How can we make meaningful decisions without having all the answers first?
